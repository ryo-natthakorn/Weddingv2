# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users
Wedding guests of Pantika & Natthakorn — Thai and English speaking family and friends, opening a
shared link on their phones (414px-class). The age range is wide: close friends in their 30s
through grandparents. Their job: feel the invitation, get the date and venue, and reply. The
couple are the clients, not the users; the card speaks for them.

## Product Purpose
A single-page interactive wedding e-card for the wedding on 22 November 2026 at SailomSangdad
Homey Studio, Bangkok. It tells the couple's story (film-roll galleries, their proposal song
"Pantika"), delivers the practical details (venue, program, dress code), and collects RSVPs into
a Google Sheet. Success: a guest scrolls once, feels personally invited, RSVPs — and then
discovers the gift envelope (ใส่ซอง), the gentle next step the couple most wants remembered.

## Positioning
A hand-made invitation that feels like visiting Pantika & Natthakorn at home — their story, their
song, one warm scroll from hello to RSVP.

## Conversion & proof
- Primary CTA: the RSVP form (3 fields, on-card, no redirect). Secondary: the gift envelope,
  revealed right after RSVP confirmation via smooth scroll.
- The line a guest remembers after 10 seconds: "I have to RSVP — they wrote their own song."
- Belief ladder: (1) this is really from P&N, made for me → (2) this wedding matters to them and
  my presence matters → (3) replying takes half a minute and helps them → RSVP → (4) the envelope
  is there if I want to give.
- Proof on hand: the personal photo film-roll + pre-wedding roll (src galleries), the proposal
  song Pantika (YouTube player), both families named on the card. No external proof needed —
  the story is the proof.

## Brand Personality
Warm, handcrafted, personal. The card should feel like being welcomed into the couple's home —
intimate, cozy, close-family energy. Voice is affectionate and unhurried, in both Thai and
English; Thai is the default and never an afterthought.

## Anti-references
- Corporate event pages (Eventbrite/conference energy: agendas, registration UX, badge language).
- Generic wedding-template builders (stock florals, script fonts, pastel clichés).
- Minimal-to-a-fault design — restraint that reads cold or impersonal is a failure here.
  (Note: richness and interactivity were deliberately NOT rejected — playful moments are welcome
  as long as they stay personal.)

## Design Principles
1. Handmade over templated — every element should feel authored by the couple (film rolls,
   envelope, their song), never assembled from a builder.
2. Warmth carries the brand — intimacy comes from storytelling, photography, and copy; color
   and ornament support it, they don't substitute for it.
3. Elder-proof by default — generous type, strong contrast, simple gestures with visible
   fallbacks; a grandparent completes the RSVP unaided on a phone.
4. One scroll, one story — the card reads top-to-bottom as a single continuous narrative
   (continuous background, no hard section breaks), ending at RSVP → gift.
5. Delight is discovered, not announced — petal trails, film-roll drags, and the envelope reward
   curiosity quietly; nothing flashes, nothing demands.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
"Comfortable for elders" is the bar: generous text sizes, ≥4.5:1 body contrast, tap targets
≥44px, every drag gesture keyboard/tap-operable, prefers-reduced-motion respected everywhere
(already implemented across sections). Thai typography must render correctly in all reveals.
